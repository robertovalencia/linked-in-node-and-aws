Reviewed 27 October 2026. Recommendation: **choose phase A, hardened EC2/SSM, for the first separately approved 24-hour exercise**. Its expected platform estimate is $3.21; a separate one-hour restore host adds about $0.11 before its extra requests/logs. This is a recommendation for the owner's cost/topology decision. It authorizes no provisioning, paid-service enablement, upgrade, public ingress or destructive operation. ## Material defects found and repaired during review

1. **Kafka could disappear from the managed-platform comparison.** Every private ECS/RDS alternative now retains and pays for a private EC2 Kafka host, its disk and its management network. Even the two-AZ task/RDS option still has a broker failure boundary. An ALB on the original host buys ingress/TLS teaching, not application/data availability or network-level database isolation.
2. **Higher MSK use exceeded the original storage allocation.** Five GB/hour with 24-hour retention is 120 GB logical data. Twenty GiB/broker cannot hold complete RF2/RF3 copies. The model now reserves 30% headroom and rounds to 160 GiB per broker. Two small brokers then cost $3.24/24h or $97.23/720h, before client network/logging. This fixes capacity accounting, not sustained t3 throughput. Platform high-use API traffic is explicitly distinct from this independent streaming ingest case; a fixed 40-GiB baseline is not asserted to hold the streaming dataset.
3. **A generic free inbound transfer SKU could erase charged cross-AZ traffic.** The final $0.01 charged-side input uses `USE2-DataTransfer-Regional-Bytes`, with two charged sides where applicable and explicit ALB-target/RDS-standby exceptions. The earlier temporarily inconsistent rate/ledger pair was regenerated. AWS's [load-balancer transfer explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/networking-and-content-delivery/exploring-data-transfer-costs-for-classic-and-application-load-balancers/) and [billing meter guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cur/latest/userguide/cur-data-transfers-charges.html) support this distinction.
4. **Private DNS was an unpriced dependency.** Private ECS alternatives now include five/ten Cloud Map registrations and a private hosted zone, with full nonprorated zone-month charges. This is a proposed implementation choice, not evidence of working service discovery. Public domain/DNS, chosen alert delivery and other named add-ons remain explicit decision-dependent exclusions.
5. **Endpoint counting and account permissions needed qualification.** The comparison now names ECR API/DKR, Logs, Secrets, SSM and ssmmessages, multiplies endpoint hours by active AZ, and uses the free S3 gateway. The retained Kafka host explains the extra SSM endpoints. No-NAT requires a maintained prebuilt installation/patch route and permitted same-region log writes. Equivalent API-traffic break-even is 0.285714 GB/hour for one AZ or 0.571429 GB/hour for two AZs; this is not a comparison of arbitrary internet access.

## Largest remaining gap and release boundary

**There is no owner-selected and cost-approved hardened topology, source-bound final apply plan, or actual recovery/runtime evidence for that topology.** The baseline preview cannot approve phase A improvements, ALB exposure or ECS/RDS migration. This is the largest remaining execution gap; a complete proposal cannot close it through documentation or account read access.

The cost/design packet is sufficiently concrete for that choice after the repairs above: it itemizes 24/720-hour gross scenarios, prices the retained broker and DNS dependencies, separates optional MSK and add-ons, and carries security/recovery/rollback/teardown gates. This finding accepts the packet as decision support only; it does not certify operational readiness or grant permission to run it.

After the owner chooses the scope and gross allowance, implement that scope, finalize alert delivery and any DNS/retention inputs, generate a fresh plan with exact replacement/public-network actions and updated costs, and obtain approval for the concrete result before provisioning. Then prove the proposed gates: least privilege, negative reachability/TLS/ownership checks, all 48 cloud acceptance results tied to source/target, broker outage/replay/deduplication, alarm transition/delivery, isolated restore with observed RPO/RTO, failed-release rollback and post-destroy inventory/residual billing checks. Image rollback cannot undo destructive schema changes, deleted topics or lost post-backup writes.

Defer B until an ingress/TLS lesson, owned domain and approved client CIDRs justify its extra exposure/cost. Defer C until separate service lifecycle/database recovery is the goal and the complete network/DNS/broker bill is accepted. MSK remains a comparison-only isolated learning option under the present explicit policy denial; Serverless' $18/day cluster floor makes it a poor idle-cost choice. Retain no availability or cost-cap claim beyond what is actually tested and measured.
